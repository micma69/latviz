Title      : Herramienta de software para la visualización de la criptografía basada en retículas
link       : https://www.polibits.cidetec.ipn.mx/ojs/index.php/polibits/article/viewFile/5089/pdf

Summary

This research was aimed to design and develop a lattice-based cryptographic algorithms visualization tool, especially the mathematical processess behind these algorithms. Just like our paper, specifically my part, the concern which becomes the background of this research is the fact that Shor's algorithm, a quantum cryptanalysis computer, can penetrate public-key based cryptographic defensive systems such as RSA. And as the response, post-quantum cryptography, in which the lattice concept is heavily used, is currently developed by the cryptographers worldwide. This also provokes the development of newer cryptanalysis techniques that can penetrate post-quantum cryptographic algorithms, such as LLL and BKZ, in which both are basis reduction techniques. The problem is, many tools only show the final result and not the step-by=step process, graphically. 

The tool which was developed by this research, was created with Python and it offered lattice visualizations up to 5 dimensions. And it can visually implement LLL reduction up to 3D. Consider how complex the dimensions of 4 and more, the researchers used tesseract and penteract for the visualization of 4D and 5D lattices. And a specific technique called "tarugo" (maybe it's a Spanish term). This technique, in which I haven't yet understand it fully, but it's basically like slices and not a full visualization of these higher dimensional objects, is developed in order to minimize the computational cost as well (probably) ease the visualization for the users and the researchers/developers themselves. 

The result of this tool is not bad, I think. The LLL results are accurate, proved by other tools such as Mathematica. As what I expected before, the overall processes for 4D and 5D dimensions are slower, because of the more complex visualization processes.
